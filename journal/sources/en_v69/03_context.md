## 3. Research context: Hybrid bonding technology and industry

Before the cross-stage technology convergence discussed in Section 2 is analyzed empirically, this section examines the technical characteristics of hybrid bonding, the object of analysis (Section 3.1), as well as the interdependence between fabrication-process and bonding technologies and related industry trends (Section 3.2). [Hybrid bonding serves here as an empirical setting for observing how knowledge from vertically separated manufacturing stages is integrated within inventions.]{.mark}

### 3.1 Technical characteristics of hybrid bonding

Hybrid bonding is a technology that brings together two planar surfaces with copper pads embedded in a dielectric, first bonds the dielectric at room temperature, and then bonds the copper pads through low-temperature annealing, thereby forming permanent electrical connections. Because no solder bumps are used, the interconnect pitch shrinks to below 10 µm, a pitch that is difficult to achieve with microbump flip-chip technology, and to below 1 µm at the research stage (Lau, 2021, 2022). Depending on what is bonded, the technology takes two main forms. Wafer-to-wafer (W2W) bonding joins entire wafers and was first commercialized in CMOS image sensors and 3D NAND. Die-to-wafer (D2W) bonding joins individual dies to a wafer and is required for chiplets, HBM, and 3D logic. ### 3.2 Interdependence between fabrication-process and bonding technologies

Because of these technical characteristics, fabrication-process and bonding technologies are closely interdependent in hybrid bonding. In both W2W and D2W bonding, the steps that determine yield, namely chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) to sub-nanometer surface roughness, plasma surface activation, particle control, and precise alignment, belong to the semiconductor fabrication-process class (H01L21) of the CPC (Section 4.1, Table 1). Accordingly, the enabling processes in Figure 1 correspond mainly to the fabrication-process class (H01L21), and the bonding configurations and interconnect structures correspond mainly to the bonding and interconnect class (H01L24). This interdependence is also evident in the actual process flow. Figure 2 shows the process flow of D2W hybrid bonding: before the dies are singulated and bonded, interlayer dielectric deposition, damascene etching, copper barrier and seed deposition, copper plating, and CMP are performed in sequence, all of which are processes and equipment that have been used in the front end. [A hybrid bonding invention therefore has to combine front-end knowledge of planarization, surface chemistry, and alignment with back-end knowledge of die handling, stacking, and packaging, knowledge that has long been held by different stages of production and often by different firms.]{.mark}

The industry landscape also reflects this technological interdependence. An independent industry analysis of hybrid bonding patents identifies TSMC, Adeia, YMTC, Intel, and Samsung as the applicants leading patent activity (KnowMade, 2024). That licensing firms holding foundational technologies, foundries operating their own advanced packaging, and memory manufacturers jointly lead patent activity shows that bonding technology is no longer the exclusive domain of back-end specialists. [The knowledge required for the technology is thus spread across organizations that the vertical division of labor had separated.]{.mark} These technological and industrial characteristics suggest that hybrid bonding is a suitable case for analyzing cross-stage technology convergence[ as the integration of knowledge across process stages]{.mark}.
