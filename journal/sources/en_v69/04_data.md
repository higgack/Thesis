## 4. Data and methods

### 4.1 Data collection

The data for this study were extracted from the 2025 edition of PATSTAT Global of the EPO (European Patent Office, 2025). Applications carrying at least one symbol of the CPC H01L21 (semiconductor fabrication processes) or H01L24 (semiconductor bonding and interconnect technology) classes were first retrieved as candidates, and only those whose titles contained at least one keyword related to hybrid bonding (*hybrid bonding*, *direct bonding*, *Cu–Cu*, *copper to copper*, or *metal-oxide bond*) were then selected (Table 1). The resulting sample consists of 5,277 application–CPC pairs, or 928 applications filed between 1968 and 2024. The sample is therefore not the universe of hybrid bonding patents but the set of related applications identified by this search query; applications whose titles do not contain a relevant term are not included. This broad sample was also designed to capture not only contemporary hybrid bonding but also the early direct bonding precursor technologies that formed its technological basis. Because symbols outside the two classes were not retained at the extraction stage, the regression data contain only 262 subgroup symbols (full symbols, including the detailed number after the slash, e.g., H01L 21/603): 202 of the H01L21 class and 60 of the H01L24 class. The technology cluster and technology network tables in Section 4.4, however, are based on a separate tabulation by the authors of all CPC symbols assigned to the same 928 applications (including the bonding indexing codes H01L2224 and the device assembly class H01L25) and are not used in the regression analyses. The unit of analysis is the individual patent application in PATSTAT; when the same invention was filed at several offices, each application is included as a separate observation.

**Table 1.** Search strategy for patents related to hybrid bonding.

| Step | Condition | Content |
|------------------|-----------|--------------------------------------------|
| Step 1: CPC | H01L21 | Semiconductor fabrication-process technology, including direct wafer bonding (21/18, 21/20), device isolation, interconnects, and through-substrate vias (21/76), substrate and insulating-layer treatment (21/02), and chemical mechanical polishing (21/30–21/32); also includes packaging-stage fabrication processes (21/48, 21/50–21/58, 21/60, 21/78) and processing apparatus (21/67–21/68) |
| Step 1: CPC | H01L24 | Connector structures for connecting or disconnecting semiconductor or solid-state bodies, bump, layer, and wire connections, and related connecting methods and apparatus |
| Step 2: Keyword | Title | At least one of *hybrid bonding*, *direct bonding*, *Cu–Cu*, *copper to copper*, and *metal-oxide bond* |

*Note:* Step 1 requires at least one of the two classes, and Step 2 at least one of the keywords; both conditions must be met. The indexing codes of the two classes (e.g., H01L2224) were also used in the search, but because only the symbols of the classes proper were retained in the extracted records, indexing codes are not included in the calculation of technological scope.

Three characteristics of the data constructed in this way need to be noted before the analysis. First, *hybrid bonding* is a relatively precise search term, whereas *direct bonding* has a broad range of meanings. There are 467 applications whose titles contain *hybrid bonding*, and the results re-estimated with this sample (hereafter the *hybrid bonding* sample) are reported in Section 5.4. Second, the keyword filter alone cannot screen out irrelevant uses of the search terms. Most such applications use *direct bonding* in a different sense, as in direct bonded copper (DBC) substrates (copper bonded to a ceramic substrate), copper alloys, and power modules; a few use *hybrid bonding* in the sense of chemical bonding or bonding sheets for power modules. These applications, identified from their titles, number 68 (63 for *direct bonding* and 5 for *hybrid bonding*), and the results re-estimated without them are also reported in Section 5.4 (see Appendix B for the criteria and distribution). Third, because the extracted records do not include PATSTAT's patent family identifiers, duplicates at the family level cannot be removed directly, so the same invention filed at several offices is counted as several applications. As a sensitivity analysis, applications with identical titles are treated as one duplicate group, and the results obtained by keeping only the earliest application in each title group are reported in Section 5.4. The 928 applications comprise 473 unique titles, of which 149 were filed at two or more offices.

### 4.2 Operational definitions and measurement

Measuring cross-stage technology convergence with the data constructed above first requires distinguishing among the theoretical concept, the observed phenomenon, and the operational indicator (Table 2). In this study, the theoretical concept is cross-stage technology convergence, the observed phenomenon is the inclusion of fabrication-process technology and bonding and interconnect technology in a single application, and the operational indicator is the co-classification of H01L21 and H01L24. [From a knowledge perspective, the two classes represent distinct knowledge domains, fabrication-process knowledge and bonding and interconnect knowledge; a bonding-only or process-only application draws on knowledge from one domain, whereas an integrated application integrates knowledge from both.]{.mark} H01L21 is not, however, regarded as the front end itself, nor H01L24 as the back end itself. This is because, in addition to wafer fabrication, H01L21 also covers processing apparatus and packaging-stage fabrication processes such as the manufacture of package parts, mounting and encapsulation, lead attachment, and dicing; H01L24 likewise does not coincide with the boundary of the back-end industry in the organizational sense. The co-classification of the two classes can nevertheless serve as a proxy because many of the wafer preparation, surface treatment, planarization, and related fabrication processes that determine the performance of hybrid bonding are included in H01L21, whereas bonding and interconnect technologies are concentrated in H01L24. The 1,636 H01L21 symbol records in the sample consist of 905 for wafer fabrication (all other H01L21 subgroups; 55.3%), 328 for processing apparatus (20.0%), and 403 for packaging-stage processes (24.6%). The results re-estimated under a narrow definition that excludes packaging-stage processes from fabrication processes are presented in Section 5.4; the conclusions of the regression analyses were unchanged.

**Table 2.** Definitions at each conceptual level and their role in this study.

| Level | Concept | Role in this study |
|------------|-----------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Technological system | Cross-stage technology convergence [(integration of knowledge across process stages)]{.mark} | Core theoretical phenomenon |
| Invention | Boundary-spanning invention [(knowledge-integrating invention)]{.mark} | Object of analysis |
| Measurement | H01L21–H01L24 co-classification | Operational indicator (proxy) |
| Patent attribute | Number of CPC subgroups within the two classes | Technological diversity (within-class technological scope) |
| Technological structure | Core technologies (H01L24 symbols assigned to 200 or more applications) | Identified through the technology cluster and network (Section 4.4) |

CPC symbols are assigned by examiners according to the technical content of an invention and do not always match the wording of the claims. Hereafter, therefore, statements that an application "contains" a certain technology or "is classified" in it mean that a symbol of the corresponding class has been assigned to the application; they are not statements about the legal scope of the claims.

### 4.3 Definition of variables

Following these operational definitions, the variables used in the analysis are defined as shown in Table 3. Technological scope (breadth) is the number of distinct subgroup symbols of the H01L21 and H01L24 classes assigned to an application. It applies, within the two classes, Lerner's (1994) approach of measuring patent scope by the number of classification symbols; it represents the diversity of the technical elements (CPC subgroups) assigned to an application within the two classes and measures neither the extent to which the application extends into fields beyond semiconductors nor the legal scope of its claims. However, the co-classification type and technological scope are derived from the same CPC information and are therefore mechanically linked: an integrated application by definition carries at least one H01L21 symbol and at least one H01L24 symbol, so its minimum breadth is two, whereas the minimum breadth of a single-class type is one. To mitigate this difference in mechanical minimums, a type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope was constructed separately by subtracting one symbol for each class that an application carries (breadth_adj = breadth − I\[has H01L21\] − I\[has H01L24\], where I\[·\] is an indicator function that equals 1 if the condition holds and 0 otherwise). For example, an integrated application with two H01L21 symbols and one H01L24 symbol has a breadth of 3 and a breadth_adj of 1, and a bonding-only application with only four H01L24 symbols has a breadth of 4 and a breadth_adj of 3. The variable breadth_adj measures the additional CPC diversity that remains after the minimum classification information establishing the type is excluded; because it reflects the type information of the dependent variable, it is used only as an exploratory robustness indicator. The co-classification type (tech_cat) classifies each application as bonding-only (only H01L24 symbols), integrated (symbols of both classes), or process-only (only H01L21 symbols); the integrated type, to which symbols of both classes are assigned together, is the measure of boundary spanning. Fabrication-process classification (has_process) is an indicator that equals 1 if an application carries at least one H01L21 symbol and 0 otherwise; it thus pools the integrated and process-only types. Filing year (yc) is the number of years elapsed since 1968, the first filing year in the sample, and filing period (period) has three categories: 1968–2014, grouped into a single period because filings were sparse, and 2015–2019 and 2020–2024 thereafter. Core technology (core) equals 1 if an application carries at least one of the four core bonding symbols identified in Section 4.4 (24/03, 24/05, 24/08, and 24/80). Filing office (ccode) is the patent office or filing route through which an application was filed and has eight levels. Because filing periods and technological composition differ across filing offices, filing office is included as a control variable in all models; its coefficients, however, are not interpreted, because differences between offices are not the subject of this study.

| breadth | Number of distinct CPC subgroups of the H01L21 and H01L24 classes assigned to an application (within-class CPC scope) | Count, 1–21 | Technological diversity within the two classes, not total CPC scope or the legal scope of the claims |
| breadth_adj | Type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope: number of additional CPC subgroups remaining after one symbol is subtracted for each class the application carries (breadth − 1 for single-class types and breadth − 2 for the integrated type; Section 5.4) | Count, 0–19 | Exploratory indicator that mitigates the mechanical difference in the minimum number of CPC symbols by type; not an independent measure of scope because it uses type information |

### 4.4 Identification of core technologies: Technology cluster and technology network

To measure core technology (core), one of the variables defined above, and to analyze shifts in the structural center of convergence, the core technologies of hybrid bonding must first be specified. This section therefore identifies them with two methods. The technology cluster method looks for the symbols that many applications share, that is, the essential elements of the technology, and the technology network method treats pairs of symbols assigned to the same application as links to capture how the elements combine (Breschi et al., 2003; Sternitzke et al., 2008). [In knowledge terms, the core technologies are the core knowledge elements of the hybrid bonding architecture, and the network shows how knowledge elements are combined.]{.mark}

**Technology cluster.** Table 4 lists the 11 symbols, among all CPC symbols assigned to the 928 applications, that are assigned to 200 or more applications; the description of each symbol summarizes its official CPC definition. The symbols assigned to the most applications are 24/80, connection methods using bonding pads ("bonding areas" in CPC terminology; 529 applications, 57.0%), and 24/08, the structure of bonding pads after connection (513 applications, 55.3%), followed by 2224/80895, direct bonding between electrically conductive surfaces (504 applications), and 2224/80896, direct bonding between electrically insulating surfaces (488 applications). Read together, these symbols describe hybrid bonding within the classification system itself: **hybrid bonding is a technology that forms copper-based bonding pads (2224/05647) flush with the surface (2224/80357) and directly bonds the metal (2224/80895) and the dielectric (2224/80896) together (24/08, 24/80) to stack chips vertically without bumps (2224/08145, 25/0657).** What distinguishes hybrid bonding from other bonding methods is the combination in which metal–metal direct bonding and dielectric–dielectric direct bonding are assigned together to a single application.

**Technology network.** Whereas the technology cluster shows the frequency of individual symbols, the technology network shows how symbols combine. The technology network consists of CPC symbols as nodes and of undirected links between any two different symbols assigned to the same application, and the weight of a link is the number of applications to which both symbols are assigned. Table 5 presents the ten pairs with the largest weights. The strongest link is the pair formed by the direct bonding of conductive surfaces and the direct bonding of insulating surfaces (460 applications), so the principle of hybrid bonding, which bonds both the metal and the dielectric, is directly reflected in the strength of the link. The remaining nine pairs are likewise all combinations of two of the five symbols 24/08, 24/80, 2224/80895, 2224/80896, and 2224/08145. **Core technologies in the regression data.** Because indexing codes are not included in the regression data, the core technologies used in the analysis are selected from among the H01L24 (bonding and interconnect classification) symbols in the regression data. Figure 4 shows the network of symbols assigned to 30 or more applications in the regression data. The four H01L24 symbols assigned to 200 or more applications in Table 4 (24/80, 24/08, 24/05, and 24/03) are also the four symbols with the greatest connection strength in this network (3,235, 3,140, 2,518, and 1,778; the connection strength of a symbol is the sum of the numbers of applications in which it is assigned together with other symbols). The six pairs formed by these four symbols (186–439 applications) are also the six strongest links in the entire network. Connection strengths and the rankings of links were calculated over all symbols and links in the regression data, regardless of the display threshold of the figure. Connection using layer connectors (24/83), the fifth most frequent symbol by number of applications, is assigned to 164 applications and thus falls short of the 200-application threshold. In the CPC scheme, all four symbols belong to the groups concerning bonding pads. Under the parent group of connection methods (24/80), methods using bump (24/81), layer (24/83), and wire (24/85) connectors are classified separately as subgroups. This study therefore defines these four symbols as the core technologies of hybrid bonding.

### 4.5 Analytical methods

Based on these variables and core technologies, this study uses three models according to the measurement scale of the dependent variable and the analysis required by each research question (Table 6). The unit of analysis is the patent application aggregated by application identifier, and filing office dummies are included as control variables in all models. The explanatory variables of the fabrication-process classification model and the co-classification type model (928 applications) are technological scope (breadth) and filing year (yc), and those of the core technology model (763 applications) are core technology (core), filing period (period), and their interaction.

**Table 6.** Roles of the three regression models.

| Analytical question | Dependent variable | Scale | Model | Reported measure | Research question |
|--------------|--------------|------------|-------------|-------------|--------|
| Does an application carry at least one fabrication-process classification? | has_process | Binary, 0/1 | Binary logit | Odds ratio (OR) | Supplementary analysis for RQ1 and RQ2 |
| To which of the three co-classification types does an application belong? | tech_cat | Unordered, 3 categories | Multinomial logit (base = bonding-only) | Relative risk ratio (RRR) | RQ1 and RQ2 |
| Does an application with bonding symbols also carry fabrication-process symbols? | Integrated or not (applications with H01L24 symbols) | Binary, 0/1 | Binary logit (core technology × filing period) | Period-specific odds ratio (OR) | RQ3 |

**Fabrication-process classification model: Binary logit (supplementary analysis).** The variable has_process equals 1 if an application carries at least one symbol of the H01L21 (fabrication-process classification) class and 0 otherwise. The 425 integrated and 165 process-only applications take the value 1, and the 338 bonding-only applications take the value 0. Because the dependent variable has only two states, the model is estimated as a binary logit that constrains probabilities to lie between 0 and 1 (log\[P/(1 − P)\] = β₀ + β₁·breadth + β₂·yc + Σγ~k~·ccode~k~), and the results are reported as odds ratios (ORs). An OR is a multiplier of the odds, not of the probability. For example, an OR of 1.5 means that a one-unit increase in an explanatory variable multiplies the odds of having a fabrication-process classification by 1.5; it should not be interpreted as a 50% higher probability. This model is a supplementary analysis that groups integrated and process-only applications as applications with a fabrication-process classification to examine in what kinds of applications fabrication-process classifications are generally observed; it is not the main analysis.

**Co-classification type model: Multinomial logit.** Because the three categories of tech_cat represent different CPC configurations rather than an order of magnitude, a multinomial logit (McFadden, 1974) is used instead of an ordered logit. With bonding-only as the base category, the relative likelihood of the integrated type, in which a fabrication-process classification appears together with bonding, and that of the process-only type, in which only fabrication processes appear without a bonding classification, can each be estimated relative to applications carrying only bonding symbols. The results are reported as relative risk ratios (RRRs) for integrated versus bonding-only and for process-only versus bonding-only. An RRR of 1.4 means that a one-unit increase in an explanatory variable multiplies the relative risk of the given type versus bonding-only by 1.4; it does not mean that the absolute probability of that type changes by the same proportion. This model is the main model for analyzing the relationship between technological scope and the integrated type and that between filing year and the process-only type.

**Interpretive constraint of including technological scope in the logit models.** Technological scope and the co-classification type are derived from the same H01L21 and H01L24 CPC information. Because an integrated application must carry at least one symbol from each class, its minimum breadth is two, whereas that of a single-class type is one. Sensitivity to this difference in minimums is checked with the type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope (breadth_adj) of Section 4.3, but because this indicator also uses type information, its use is limited to exploratory robustness analysis.

**Core technology model: Binary logit.** Because the core technologies are bonding symbols, process-only applications cannot, by definition, contain them. The core technology model is therefore a binary logit estimated on the 763 applications carrying H01L24 symbols (425 integrated and 338 bonding-only), with integrated status as the dependent variable. The model analyzes which of the applications carrying bonding symbols also carry fabrication-process symbols, and it uses the same sample as the alternative-exclusion sensitivity analysis in Appendix A. The model is log\[P/(1 − P)\] = β₀ + β₁·core + Σδ~t~·period~t~ + Σθ~t~·(core × period~t~) + Σγ~k~·ccode~k~. The odds ratio of the core technologies is exp(β₁) for applications filed in 1968–2014 and exp(β₁ + θ~t~) for 2015–2019 and 2020–2024, and exp(θ~t~) is the ratio of the odds ratio in each period to that in 1968–2014. If applications containing the core technologies initially had an integrated-type advantage that later diminished, exp(β₁) would exceed 1 and θ~t~ would be negative. The 95% confidence intervals of the period-specific odds ratios are calculated from the standard error of the sum of the two coefficients, and the standard errors are estimated to be robust to heteroskedasticity. Filing year is divided into three periods rather than entered linearly in order to approximate the stages of the technology cycle (Lee et al., 2017). The results of controlling for the number of bonding symbols other than the core symbols are presented in Section 5.4.

**Supplementary indicators of convergence.** To examine shifts in the center of convergence at a broader level than individual symbols, two indicators are calculated for each period. First, the extent to which each of the three fabrication-process domains of Section 4.2 (wafer fabrication, processing apparatus, and packaging-stage processes) is assigned to the same application as bonding symbols (H01L24), that is, their coupling strength, is measured with the Jaccard coefficient (Jaccard, 1912). The Jaccard coefficient is the number of applications carrying symbols of both domains divided by the number of applications carrying symbols of either; unlike a simple co-occurrence frequency, it corrects for differences in the number of applications across domains. Second, period-specific technology networks are constructed in the same way as the regression data network of Section 4.4; symbols with a large connection strength are regarded as hubs that are most frequently assigned together with other symbols, and symbols with high betweenness centrality are regarded as linking symbols that connect different symbols. Betweenness centrality is the extent to which a symbol lies on the shortest paths between other pairs of symbols (Freeman, 1977) and is calculated without considering link weights. [In knowledge terms, the Jaccard coefficient indicates how strongly each fabrication-process knowledge domain is integrated with bonding knowledge, and betweenness centrality indicates which knowledge domains link the two sides, that is, the locus of integration.]{.mark}

**Predicted probabilities.** Because ORs and RRRs are ratio measures, they do not convey intuitively the magnitude of actual changes in probability. For the multinomial logit, average predicted probabilities are therefore also presented; these are obtained by varying only the explanatory variable of interest (technological scope or filing year) while holding the other variables at each application's observed values and averaging over the whole sample (Sections 5.1 and 5.2). For the core technology model, the share of integrated applications by period and by the presence of the core technologies is also presented (Section 5.3).

**Robustness and principles of interpretation.** Four alternative specifications address specific concerns: the *hybrid bonding* sample checks whether the results depend on the broad sample that includes precursor technologies; the sample excluding irrelevant uses, whether other meanings of the search terms distort the results; the narrow fabrication-process definition, whether the results are sensitive to how the boundary of H01L21 is drawn; and the same-title deduplicated sample, the influence of repeated or similar inventions. For the core technology model, a model that controls for the number of bonding symbols other than the core symbols and a definition that restricts the core technologies to the most strongly linked pair of core symbols (24/08 and 24/80) are added. The *hybrid bonding* sample is not used for the core technology model because it contains only 12 bonding applications without the core technologies up to 2019. To address the possibility that observations are not independent because applications with the same title recur at several offices, significance is recalculated with cluster-robust standard errors that treat each title as a cluster; in this approach, the coefficients are unchanged, and only the standard errors differ. Regarding the independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) assumed by the multinomial logit, Appendix A presents, instead of a formal test, an alternative-exclusion sensitivity analysis that excludes the process-only type, estimates a binary logit of integrated versus bonding-only, and checks whether the substantive interpretation holds. The results are interpreted at the 5% significance level, but not on the basis of a single p-value alone; rather, the consistency of the direction, effect size, and significance of the coefficients across the baseline model, the alternative samples and definitions, and the clustered standard errors is assessed comprehensively. A p-value of 0.05 or greater does not prove the absence of an effect but indicates a lack of clear evidence at the 5% level, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are presented for the key effect ratios. All coefficients are interpreted not as causal effects but as conditional associations that control for the observed variables.
