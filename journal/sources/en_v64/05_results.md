## 5. Results

This section presents the results in the order of the three research questions (Sections 5.1–5.3) and tests their robustness in Section 5.4.

### 5.1 Level and types of technology convergence (RQ1)

This subsection first examines to what extent cross-stage convergence occurs and what forms it takes. Of all 928 applications, the integrated type accounted for the largest share, with 425 applications (45.8%), and its share reached 37.2% even under the narrow definition that excludes packaging-stage processes from fabrication processes (Section 5.4). In other words, the convergence of the two process stages is not an exceptional phenomenon in patents related to hybrid bonding.

To identify the patents in which this convergence appears, Table 7 first presents the results of a binary logit of whether an application carries fabrication-process symbols (H01L21). Technological scope shows a strong positive association with carrying fabrication-process symbols: with each additional detailed classification, the odds of carrying a fabrication-process symbol increased by about 27% (OR 1.267, p \< 0.01). The area under the ROC curve (AUC), which indicates the discriminatory power of the model, is 0.707, which lies at the lower end of the acceptable range according to the criteria of Hosmer et al. (2013). The coefficient of filing year is discussed in Section 5.2.

Next, Table 8 reports the results of the multinomial logit as relative risk ratios of the integrated and process-only types, with bonding-only as the base category; this is the main analysis of which patents cross the boundary. Figure 5 shows the predicted probabilities of the three types from this model as technological scope and filing year vary (with the other variables held at each application's observed values and then averaged).

**Applications that include more diverse detailed technical elements in the fabrication-process and bonding domains were more likely to be integrated.** With each additional detailed classification, the relative risk of being integrated rather than bonding-only increased by 40% (RRR 1.403, p \< 0.01), and that of being process-only decreased by 10% (RRR 0.905, p \< 0.05). In terms of magnitude, the predicted probabilities for an application with a technological scope of 3 are 0.26 for integrated, 0.50 for bonding-only, and 0.24 for process-only, whereas those for an application with a technological scope of 8 are 0.68 for integrated, 0.24 for bonding-only, and 0.08 for process-only (Figure 5, left). In other words, applications with a broad scope tend to span the two domains, and those with a narrow scope tend to remain within one domain. This shows the compositional characteristic that integrated patents include more diverse detailed technologies within the selected CPC domains.

### 5.2 Temporal change in technology convergence (RQ2)

The level of convergence identified in Section 5.1 differed considerably over time. Table 9 and Figure 6 show the distribution of types across three periods defined in light of the concentration of filings. The years 1968–2014 were grouped into a single period because filings were so sparse that the 47 years produced fewer applications (179) than the following five years (209), and the years from 2015 onward were divided into five-year periods, 2015–2019 and 2020–2024. The share of process-only applications declined steadily from 43.6% in 1968–2014 to 14.8% in 2015–2019 and 10.4% in 2020–2024. The integrated share rose sharply from 31.3% to 62.2% and then fell to 44.3%, whereas the bonding-only share rose from 25.1% and 23.0% to 45.4%. This evolution of convergence can be interpreted as consisting of three phases. Up to 2014, process-only applications were the most common; in 2015–2019, the integrated share peaked at 62.2%, and the technologies of the two process stages were most often included together in a single invention. In 2020–2024, the integrated share declined, and the bonding-only share (45.4%) rose to a level similar to that of the integrated type (44.3%). Hereafter, the three phases are referred to as the formation phase, the diffusion phase, and the reconfiguration phase, respectively.

**The more recent the filing year, the more likely patents were to be classified as bonding-only rather than process-only.** In the multinomial logit of Table 8, the relative risk of the process-only type decreased by about 8.7% per year (RRR 0.913, p \< 0.01), whereas the relative risk of the integrated type showed no significant linear trend over the entire period (RRR 0.993). The predicted probability of being process-only was 0.27 for applications filed in 2010 but 0.12 for those filed in 2022; over the same period, that of being bonding-only increased from 0.31 to 0.40, whereas that of being integrated barely changed, from 0.43 to 0.47 (Figure 5, right). In the binary logit of Table 7 as well, the odds ratio of filing year was less than 1 (annual OR 0.946, p \< 0.01), so more recent applications had lower odds of carrying fabrication-process symbols, and this decrease stems mainly from the decline of the process-only type. The same pattern is confirmed in a multinomial logit that includes period dummies instead of a linear year term. Relative to 1968–2014, the relative risk of the process-only type fell to 0.273 in 2015–2019 and 0.101 in 2020–2024 (both p \< 0.01), whereas that of the integrated type rose to 1.707 (p = 0.09) and then fell to 0.762 (not significant). In the 2010–2024 sample (N = 830), the year coefficient for the integrated type was also significantly less than 1 (RRR 0.934, p \< 0.05), and the same held for the narrow fabrication-process definition and the sample excluding irrelevant uses (Appendix A). These results show that the center of gravity of filings shifted from fabrication processes toward bonding.

Meanwhile, the association between technological scope and the integrated type persisted in all three phases. When the model is estimated separately for each period, the relative risk ratio of technological scope for the integrated type is 1.179 in the formation phase, 1.513 in the diffusion phase, and 1.414 in the reconfiguration phase, all significant at the 1% level (for the first period, filing office dummies were excluded from the estimation because many filing office cells were empty). In other words, although the share of convergence and the center of gravity of filings changed from phase to phase, the characteristic that boundary-spanning patents include more diverse detailed technologies persisted in all three phases.

### 5.3 Shift in the structural center of technology convergence (RQ3)

To understand how the phase-by-phase changes identified in Section 5.2 relate to the structure of convergence, this subsection analyzes, by period, the extent to which bonding applications containing the core technologies also carry fabrication-process symbols and the changes in the fabrication-process domains that combine with bonding symbols. Table 10 presents the results of the core technology model.

**Applications containing the core technologies were initially far more likely to be integrated than applications without them, but this difference diminished among more recent filings.** In the formation phase (up to 2014), 79.6% of the bonding applications containing the core technologies were integrated, compared with only 27.7% of those without them, and the odds ratio of the core technologies, controlling for filing office, was 11.071 (p \< 0.01). In the diffusion phase (2015–2019), applications containing the core technologies were still more often classified as integrated (77.1% vs. 57.9%; odds ratio 2.452, p \< 0.05), but the odds ratio decreased to 0.221 times that of 1968–2014 (p \< 0.05). In the reconfiguration phase (2020–2024), the integrated share of applications containing the core technologies fell to 48.4% and did not differ significantly from that of applications without them (56.9%; odds ratio 0.658, p = 0.15). The odds ratio in this period is 0.059 times that of 1968–2014 (p \< 0.01). In short, the tendency of applications containing the core technologies to cross the boundary more often was pronounced in the formation phase but was not observed in the reconfiguration phase.

The interactions (multiples relative to 1968–2014) are 0.221 for 2015–2019 (p = 0.016) and 0.059 for 2020–2024 (p \< 0.001); the joint Wald χ²(2) of the two interactions is 26.43 (p \< 0.001), and McFadden's pseudo-R² is 0.069.

The change originated in the applications containing the core technologies. The integrated share of bonding applications without the core technologies actually rose from 27.7% to 57.9% and 56.9%, whereas that of applications containing them fell from 79.6% and 77.1% to 48.4%. Over the same periods, the share of applications containing the core technologies among bonding applications rose from 53.5% to 78.7% and 88.0%, which suggests that invention converged on a single core design. The fabrication-process symbols assigned together with the core technologies also differed across periods. In integrated applications containing the core technologies, the most frequent H01L21 symbols were the joining of semiconductor bodies (21/187; 22 applications) and through-substrate vias (21/76898; 21 applications) in 2015–2019 and support using temporary substrates (21/6835; 48 applications) and temporary substrates used as encapsulation process aids (21/568; 43 applications) in 2020–2024. The latter two symbols concern processes for supporting dies and completing them as packages rather than the bonding principle itself. Taken together, these results suggest that inventions containing the core technologies initially incorporated fabrication-process knowledge far more often than other bonding inventions, that this difference is no longer observed in recent filings, and that the fabrication processes assigned together with the core technologies have shifted to peripheral processes such as die support and encapsulation.

This shift is also evident beyond individual symbols, in the fabrication-process domains as a whole and in the structure of the technology network (Table 11). In terms of the Jaccard coefficient with bonding symbols, wafer fabrication was highest in the diffusion phase and then dropped sharply (0.306 in the formation phase, 0.598 in the diffusion phase, and 0.159 in the reconfiguration phase), whereas processing apparatus (0.046, 0.059, and 0.176) and packaging-stage processes (0.194, 0.234, and 0.268) rose steadily. From the diffusion phase to the reconfiguration phase, the number of applications in which each domain's symbols were assigned together with bonding symbols decreased from 119 to 82 for wafer fabrication but increased from 11 to 89 for processing apparatus and from 43 to 139 for packaging-stage processes. Consequently, the fabrication-process domain most strongly coupled with bonding changed from wafer fabrication in the formation and diffusion phases to packaging-stage processes in the reconfiguration phase. In the technology network, the hubs and the linking symbols showed different patterns. The three symbols with the greatest connection strength were core symbols (24/80, 24/08, and 24/05) in all three periods, so the core technologies retained their position as hubs. By contrast, the symbol with the highest betweenness centrality in the entire network in the formation and diffusion phases was the joining of semiconductor bodies (21/187), and wafer fabrication symbols accounted for 53.6% and 42.5%, respectively, of total betweenness centrality. In the reconfiguration phase, this share fell to 9.0%, processing apparatus symbols accounted for 26.1%, and the five fabrication-process symbols with the highest betweenness centrality were four processing apparatus symbols (21/67121, apparatus for making assemblies such as package structures; 21/67766, mechanical parts of transfer devices; 21/67092, apparatus for mechanical treatment; and 21/6835, support using temporary substrates) and one packaging-stage process symbol (21/50). The same pattern held when betweenness centrality was recalculated with the reciprocals of the link weights as distances. In short, while the core technologies remained at the center of the network, the links connecting bonding and fabrication processes shifted from wafer fabrication to processing apparatus and packaging-stage processes.

### 5.4 Robustness checks

To check whether these results depend on the sample and variable definitions, the models were re-estimated after changing the sample and variables in the raw data. *Note:* In the clustered standard error row, the point estimates are identical to the baseline, and only the significance is based on clustering by title. The standard errors, the remaining coefficients, and the exploratory results for the type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope (breadth_adj) are presented in Appendix A.

**Independence of observations.** Even when the standard errors were recalculated with applications of the same title grouped into one cluster, both coefficients in Table 12 remained significant at the 1% level. The only coefficient that loses significance is scope → process-only (RRR 0.905, p = 0.29).

**Mechanical component of scope.** Because an integrated application by definition carries at least one symbol from each class, its scope cannot be less than two. The positive association observed for the baseline breadth may therefore partly reflect this difference in the minimum number of CPC symbols by type. When the models were re-estimated with the type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope (breadth_adj, Section 4.3), which subtracts one symbol for each class an application carries, the RRR for the integrated type decreased from 1.403 to 1.262, but its direction was maintained (Appendix A). This means that the additional CPC diversity beyond the minimum composition of each type is also associated with the integrated type.

**Same-title duplicates.** Keeping only the earliest application among applications with the same title leaves a sample of 473 applications. In this sample as well, the key coefficients of technological scope and filing year both remained significant at the 1% level.

**Definition of fabrication processes.** As noted in Section 4.2, H01L21 (fabrication-process classification) also includes packaging-stage fabrication processes. When a narrow definition that excludes these from fabrication processes is applied (excluding 20 applications with no remaining symbols, N = 908), the applications carrying fabrication-process symbols fall to 52.0% of all 928 applications, and the type distribution of the 908 applications changes to 46.8% bonding-only, 37.2% integrated, and 16.0% process-only, making the bonding-only type the most common. Thus, the descriptive finding that the integrated type is the most common depends on the definition, but even under the narrow definition, more than one-third of applications carry symbols of both domains, and the signs and significance of the two key coefficients remain unchanged.

**Precision of the search terms.** In the sample excluding the 68 irrelevant uses (N = 860), the type distribution is 47.4% integrated, 35.3% bonding-only, and 17.2% process-only, and all key coefficients are maintained. In the *hybrid bonding* sample (N = 467), the distribution is 49.5% integrated, 38.3% bonding-only, and 12.2% process-only, and the relative risk ratio of technological scope for the integrated type (1.485) remains significant at the 1% level. Because this sample contains only 30 applications filed up to 2014, however, the year coefficient is not significant (1.029). This is likely because the decline of the process-only type largely reflects a transition from wafer bonding process applications filed when *direct bonding* was the prevailing term to structural applications filed in the *hybrid bonding* era, and this sample cannot adequately capture the earlier period.

**To check whether the results of the core technology analysis are due to a particular sample or variable definition, the baseline model (Table 10) was re-estimated in six ways.** The model that controls for the number of bonding symbols other than the core symbols checks whether the effect of the core technologies merely reflects a characteristic of applications that carry many bonding symbols, and the model that restricts the core technologies to 24/08 and 24/80 checks whether the same results hold when the scope of the core technologies is narrowed. The remaining four check the independence of observations, same-title duplicates, the definition of fabrication processes, and irrelevant uses of the search terms in the same way as for the multinomial logit in Table 12. In all seven estimations in Table 13, the odds ratio for 1968–2014 exceeds 1, the odds ratio for 2020–2024 does not differ significantly from 1, and the ratio between the two (last column) is significantly less than 1. Even when the number of bonding symbols other than the core symbols is controlled for (with its own odds ratio of 1.100, p \< 0.01), the period-specific odds ratios barely change. The results are weakest in the sample excluding irrelevant uses. In this sample, the odds ratio for 1968–2014 decreases to 3.981 because 28 of the 51 irrelevant applications carrying bonding symbols were applications without the core technologies filed up to 2014, 25 of which were bonding-only. Nevertheless, the odds ratio for 1968–2014 (p \< 0.05) and the 2020–2024 interaction (0.185, p \< 0.05) are significant in this sample. However, because the odds ratio for 2015–2019 loses significance with the clustered standard errors and in the sample excluding irrelevant uses, this study does not determine conclusively whether the advantage of the core technologies ceased to be significant in the diffusion phase or in the reconfiguration phase.
