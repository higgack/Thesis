## Abstract

Front-end wafer fabrication and back-end assembly have long been separate domains of semiconductor manufacturing. Hybrid bonding, a back-end technology, achieves its required performance only with front-end fabrication capabilities, driving convergence across process stages. This study examines the level and types of cross-stage convergence in hybrid bonding patents, its change over time, and shifts in its structural center. Applications from the European Patent Office's PATSTAT database were classified by Cooperative Patent Classification symbols as bonding-only, process-only, or integrated and analyzed using multinomial and binary logit models and technology cluster and network analyses. First, integrated applications formed the largest group, and their share remained substantial even under a narrower definition excluding packaging-stage processes, so cross-stage convergence is not exceptional; applications spanning more diverse technologies were more likely to be integrated. Second, convergence evolved through three phases—formation, dominated by process-only applications; diffusion, when integrated applications peaked; and reconfiguration, when they declined and bonding-only applications increased—while the center of gravity of filings moved from fabrication processes toward bonding. Third, applications containing the core bonding technologies, identified through cluster and network analyses, were far more likely than others to carry fabrication-process classifications in the formation phase, but this gap narrowed and was insignificant in the reconfiguration phase, while the fabrication processes combined with bonding shifted from wafer fabrication to processing apparatus and packaging-stage processes. Cross-stage convergence thus appears to be a dynamic process, not a linear expansion, in which its content and center are reorganized across phases as the core design becomes entrenched.

**Keywords:** technology convergence; cross-stage convergence; hybrid bonding; advanced packaging; technology network; dominant design
