## 4. Data and methods

### 4.1 Data collection

The data were extracted from the 2025 edition of PATSTAT Global of the EPO (European Patent Office, 2025). The search proceeded in two steps (Table A1 in Appendix A). Applications carrying at least one symbol of the CPC H01L21 (semiconductor fabrication processes) or H01L24 (semiconductor bonding and interconnect technology) classes were first retrieved, and only those whose titles contained at least one keyword related to hybrid bonding (*hybrid bonding*, *direct bonding*, *Cu–Cu*, *copper to copper*, or *metal-oxide bond*) were then selected. The final sample consists of 928 applications filed between 1968 and 2024, or 5,277 application–CPC pairs. The sample is not the universe of hybrid bonding patents but the set of related applications identified by this search query, and it was designed broadly to capture not only contemporary hybrid bonding but also the early direct bonding precursor technologies on which it builds. Because symbols outside the two classes were not retained at the extraction stage, the regression data contain 262 subgroup symbols, 202 of the H01L21 class and 60 of the H01L24 class, whereas the technology cluster and network tables in Section 4.4 are based on a separate tabulation of all CPC symbols assigned to the same 928 applications (including the bonding indexing codes H01L2224 and the device assembly class H01L25). The unit of analysis is the individual patent application.

Three characteristics of these data are examined in the robustness analyses (Section 5.4). First, because *direct bonding* has a broader range of meanings than *hybrid bonding*, the models were re-estimated on the 467 applications whose titles contain *hybrid bonding* (hereafter the *hybrid bonding* sample). Second, 68 applications in which a search term is used in a different sense, as in direct bonded copper (DBC) substrates, in which copper is bonded to a ceramic substrate, or power modules (63 for *direct bonding* and 5 for *hybrid bonding*), were identified from their titles, and the models were re-estimated without them (Appendix A). Third, because the extracted records do not include patent family identifiers, the same invention filed at several offices is counted as several applications; this was checked with a sample that keeps only the earliest application among those sharing the same title and with standard errors clustered by title. The 928 applications comprise 473 unique titles, of which 149 were filed at two or more offices.

### 4.2 Operational definitions and measurement

In this study, the theoretical concept is cross-stage technology convergence, that is, the integration of knowledge from two process domains; the observed phenomenon is the inclusion of fabrication-process technology and bonding and interconnect technology in a single application; and the operational indicator is the co-classification of H01L21 and H01L24. From a knowledge perspective, the two classes represent distinct knowledge domains, fabrication-process knowledge and bonding and interconnect knowledge, and an integrated application is an invention that integrates knowledge from both. The two classes are not, however, regarded as the front end and the back end themselves: in addition to wafer fabrication, H01L21 covers packaging-stage fabrication processes such as mounting, encapsulation, and dicing as well as processing apparatus, and H01L24 does not coincide with the boundary of the back-end industry in the organizational sense. The co-classification of the two classes can nevertheless serve as a proxy because many of the wafer preparation, surface treatment, and planarization processes that determine the performance of hybrid bonding are included in H01L21, whereas bonding and interconnect technologies are concentrated in H01L24. The 1,636 H01L21 symbol records in the sample consist of 905 for wafer fabrication (55.3%), 328 for processing apparatus (20.0%), and 403 for packaging-stage processes (24.6%), and the models were also re-estimated under a narrow definition that excludes packaging-stage processes (Section 5.4). Because CPC symbols are assigned by examiners, statements below that an application "contains" a technology mean that a symbol of the corresponding class has been assigned to it; they are not statements about the legal scope of the claims.

### 4.3 Definition of variables

The variables are defined in Table 2. Technological scope (breadth) represents the diversity of the technical elements assigned to an application within the two classes; it measures neither the extent to which the application extends into fields beyond semiconductors nor the legal scope of its claims. Because the co-classification type and technological scope are derived from the same CPC information, the minimum breadth of an integrated application is two by definition, whereas that of a single-class type is one. To mitigate this difference in mechanical minimums, a type-minimum-adjusted CPC scope was constructed by subtracting one symbol for each class that an application carries (breadth_adj = breadth − I\[has H01L21\] − I\[has H01L24\]); because this indicator also uses type information, it is used only as an exploratory robustness indicator. Filing period (period) groups 1968–2014, when filings were sparse, into a single period and divides the subsequent years into five-year periods. Because filing periods and technological composition differ across filing offices, filing office (ccode) is included as a control variable in all models, but its coefficients are not interpreted. Technological scope averages 5.69 symbols (standard deviation 4.07): 7.89 for integrated, 4.16 for bonding-only, and 3.15 for process-only applications. By filing office, there are 382 applications at the US office, 175 at the Chinese office, 113 through the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) route, 83 at the EPO, 75 at the Taiwanese office, 58 at the Korean office, 26 at the Japanese office, and 16 at other offices. Applications were rare before 2010 but surged in the late 2010s and peaked around 2022 (Figure 2).

### 4.4 Identification of core technologies: Technology cluster and technology network

The core technologies were identified with two methods: the technology cluster method, which looks for the symbols that many applications share, and the technology network method, which treats pairs of symbols assigned to the same application as links to capture how the elements combine (Breschi et al., 2003; Sternitzke et al., 2008). In knowledge terms, the core technologies are the core knowledge elements of the hybrid bonding architecture, and the network shows how knowledge elements are combined.

Table 3 lists the 11 symbols, among all CPC symbols assigned to the 928 applications, that are assigned to 200 or more applications. The symbols assigned to the most applications are 24/80, connection methods using bonding pads ("bonding areas" in CPC terminology; 529 applications, 57.0%), and 24/08, the structure of bonding pads after connection (513 applications, 55.3%), followed by 2224/80895, direct bonding between electrically conductive surfaces (504 applications), and 2224/80896, direct bonding between electrically insulating surfaces (488 applications). Read together, these symbols define hybrid bonding within the classification system as a technology that forms copper-based bonding pads (2224/05647) flush with the surface (2224/80357) and directly bonds the metal (2224/80895) and the dielectric (2224/80896) together (24/08, 24/80) to stack chips vertically without bumps (2224/08145, 25/0657). In the technology network as well, the strongest link is the pair formed by the direct bonding of conductive surfaces and the direct bonding of insulating surfaces (460 applications), and all of the ten strongest pairs are combinations of two of the five symbols 24/08, 24/80, 2224/80895, 2224/80896, and 2224/08145 (Appendix B). The core technologies of hybrid bonding are thus concentrated in a small number of closely linked bonding symbols.

Because indexing codes are not included in the regression data, the core technologies used in the analysis were selected from among the H01L24 symbols in the regression data. The four H01L24 symbols assigned to 200 or more applications in Table 3 (24/80, 24/08, 24/05, and 24/03) are also the four symbols with the greatest connection strength (the sum of the numbers of applications in which a symbol is assigned together with other symbols) in the network of the regression data (Figure 3; 3,235, 3,140, 2,518, and 1,778), and the six pairs formed by these four symbols (186–439 applications) are the six strongest links in the entire network. In the CPC scheme, all four symbols belong to the groups concerning bonding pads: 24/03 and 24/05 cover the manufacture of bonding pads and their structure before connection, and 24/08 and 24/80 cover the structure after bonding pads have been connected to one another without separate connectors such as bumps, layers, or wires, and the corresponding connection methods. This study therefore defines these four symbols as the core technologies of hybrid bonding. Connection using layer connectors (24/83), the fifth most frequent symbol by number of applications, is assigned to 164 applications and thus falls short of the 200-application threshold. Of the 763 applications carrying H01L24 symbols (hereafter bonding applications), 620 (81.3%) contain the core technologies.

### 4.5 Analytical methods

This study uses three models, all of which include filing office dummies as control variables. The main model, the co-classification type model, is a multinomial logit (McFadden, 1974) with tech_cat as the dependent variable (N = 928). A multinomial logit is used because the three categories represent different CPC configurations rather than an order of magnitude, and the results are reported as relative risk ratios (RRRs) of the integrated and process-only types, with bonding-only as the base category. The supplementary fabrication-process classification model is a binary logit with has_process as the dependent variable, and its results are reported as odds ratios (ORs). The explanatory variables of both models are technological scope and filing year. Because ORs and RRRs are not rates of change in probability, average predicted probabilities, obtained by varying only the explanatory variable of interest while holding the other variables at each application's observed values, are also presented for the multinomial logit.

The core technology model is a binary logit estimated on the 763 bonding applications (425 integrated and 338 bonding-only) with integrated status as the dependent variable (process-only applications cannot, by definition, contain the core technologies). The model is log\[P/(1 − P)\] = β₀ + β₁·core + Σδ~t~·period~t~ + Σθ~t~·(core × period~t~) + Σγ~k~·ccode~k~. The odds ratio of the core technologies is exp(β₁) for applications filed in 1968–2014 and exp(β₁ + θ~t~) for 2015–2019 and 2020–2024, and exp(θ~t~) is the ratio of the odds ratio in each period to that in 1968–2014. If the integrated-type advantage of applications containing the core technologies diminished, the odds ratio for 1968–2014 would exceed 1 and θ~t~ would be negative. Filing year is divided into three periods in order to approximate the stages of the technology cycle (Lee et al., 2017), and the standard errors are robust to heteroskedasticity.

The shift in the locus of knowledge integration is examined with two supplementary indicators calculated by period. The first is the Jaccard coefficient (Jaccard, 1912), which measures the extent to which each of three fabrication-process domains (wafer fabrication, processing apparatus, and packaging-stage processes) is assigned to the same application as bonding symbols. The second is the period-specific technology network, in which symbols with a large connection strength are regarded as hubs and symbols with high betweenness centrality (Freeman, 1977) as linking symbols that connect different symbols; betweenness centrality is calculated without considering link weights. In knowledge terms, the Jaccard coefficient indicates how strongly each fabrication-process knowledge domain is integrated with bonding knowledge, and betweenness centrality indicates which knowledge domains link the two sides, that is, the locus of integration.

Robustness is examined with the alternative samples and definitions of Sections 4.1 and 4.2 and with standard errors clustered by title; for the core technology model, a model that controls for the number of bonding symbols other than the core symbols and a definition that restricts the core technologies to the most strongly linked pair (24/08 and 24/80) are added. The independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) assumed by the multinomial logit is checked with an alternative-exclusion sensitivity analysis (Appendix C). The results are interpreted at the 5% significance level, but the consistency of the direction, magnitude, and significance of the coefficients across the baseline model and the alternative samples and definitions is assessed comprehensively, and all coefficients are interpreted not as causal effects but as conditional associations that control for the observed variables.
