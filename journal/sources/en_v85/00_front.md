## Abstract

Front-end fabrication and back-end assembly have long been separate knowledge domains in semiconductor manufacturing. Hybrid bonding, a back-end technology, requires front-end fabrication knowledge to achieve its performance, driving convergence, or knowledge integration, across process stages. Using hybrid bonding patent applications from the European Patent Office's PATSTAT, this study examines the level and forms of cross-stage knowledge integration, its change over time, and shifts in its locus. Applications were classified as bonding-only, process-only, or integrated by their Cooperative Patent Classification co-classification, a trace of this integration, and analyzed with multinomial and binary logit models and technology cluster and network analyses. First, integrated applications formed the largest group and remained substantial under a narrower definition excluding packaging-stage processes, indicating that cross-stage convergence was not exceptional; they also spanned more diverse technologies. Second, integration evolved through three phases (formation, when process-only applications dominated; diffusion, when integrated applications peaked; and reconfiguration, when they declined and bonding-only applications increased) as the weight of filings shifted from fabrication processes toward bonding. Third, bonding applications containing the core technologies were far more likely than others to carry fabrication-process classifications in the formation phase, but this gap became nonsignificant by the reconfiguration phase, and the center of the fabrication processes combined with bonding shifted from wafer fabrication to processing apparatus and packaging-stage processes. Cross-stage convergence is thus not a linear expansion but a dynamic process whose content and center are reorganized across phases; as the core design became entrenched, knowledge integration appears to have relocated rather than disappeared.

**Keywords:** technology convergence; cross-stage convergence; hybrid bonding; advanced packaging; knowledge integration; knowledge boundaries
